# Today's Words — 2026-09-24

1. **Vivid** (VIV-id) — Very bright, clear, and full of detail.
   *Example:* She gave a vivid description of her trip to the mountains.
2. **Wary** (WAIR-ee) — Careful and cautious because you sense possible danger.
   *Example:* The hikers were wary of the loose rocks on the trail.
3. **Zealous** (ZEL-us) — Showing great energy and enthusiasm for a cause or goal.
   *Example:* The zealous volunteers cleaned the entire park in one afternoon.
4. **Yearn** (YURN) — To want something very much, often with a feeling of longing.
   *Example:* After months away, he began to yearn for his hometown.
5. **Adept** (uh-DEPT) — Very skilled or good at doing something.
   *Example:* She is adept at solving tricky math problems quickly.
6. **Ample** (AM-pul) — More than enough; plenty.
   *Example:* We had ample time to finish the exam before the bell rang.
7. **Banal** (buh-NAHL) — So ordinary and overused that it is boring.
   *Example:* The movie's ending felt banal and predictable.
8. **Candor** (KAN-dur) — Honesty and openness in what you say.
   *Example:* The coach spoke with candor about the team's weaknesses.
9. **Deplete** (dih-PLEET) — To reduce the amount of something by using it up.
   *Example:* Long hours of studying can deplete your energy if you don't rest.
10. **Elude** (ih-LOOD) — To escape or avoid someone or something in a skillful way.
    *Example:* The answer to the puzzle continued to elude the students.
11. **Fervent** (FUR-vent) — Having or showing very strong and sincere feeling.
    *Example:* The fans were fervent supporters of their school team.
12. **Garrulous** (GAIR-uh-lus) — Talking a lot, especially about unimportant things.
    *Example:* Our garrulous neighbor chatted with us for over an hour.
13. **Hone** (HOHN) — To improve or perfect a skill over time through practice.
    *Example:* She practices daily to hone her public speaking skills.
14. **Impetuous** (im-PECH-oo-us) — Acting quickly without thinking carefully first.
    *Example:* His impetuous decision to skip practice upset the coach.
15. **Jaded** (JAY-did) — No longer excited about something because you have seen or done it too much.
    *Example:* After years of the same routine, he felt jaded about school trips.
16. **Latent** (LAY-tent) — Present but hidden or not yet developed.
    *Example:* The coach noticed a latent talent for running in the new student.
17. **Mundane** (mun-DAYN) — Ordinary and not exciting; everyday.
    *Example:* Washing dishes is a mundane chore, but someone has to do it.
18. **Naive** (nah-EEV) — Lacking experience or judgment; too trusting or simple.
    *Example:* It was naive of him to believe the rumor without checking the facts.
19. **Opulent** (OP-yuh-lent) — Extremely luxurious and expensive-looking.
    *Example:* The hotel lobby had an opulent chandelier and marble floors.
20. **Placate** (PLAY-kayt) — To make someone calmer or less angry.
    *Example:* She tried to placate her upset friend by apologizing right away.
