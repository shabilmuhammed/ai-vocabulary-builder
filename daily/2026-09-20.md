# Today's Words — 2026-09-20

1. **Ambiguous** (am-BIG-yoo-us) — Having more than one possible meaning; unclear.
   *Example:* The instructions were so ambiguous that nobody knew where to line up.

2. **Brittle** (BRIT-ul) — Hard but easily broken; fragile.
   *Example:* The old, brittle pages of the book tore as she turned them.

3. **Coherent** (koh-HEER-ent) — Logical and clear; easy to understand.
   *Example:* He gave a coherent explanation of how the experiment worked.

4. **Diminish** (dih-MIN-ish) — To make or become smaller or less.
   *Example:* Her fear began to diminish once she started the exam.

5. **Enhance** (en-HANSS) — To improve the quality or value of something.
   *Example:* Extra practice will enhance your chances of winning the debate.

6. **Feasible** (FEE-zuh-bul) — Possible to do easily or conveniently.
   *Example:* The teacher asked if the group project plan was feasible.

7. **Grim** (GRIM) — Serious, gloomy, or unpleasant.
   *Example:* The weather forecast looked grim for the school sports day.

8. **Hinder** (HIN-der) — To create difficulty for someone, delaying progress.
   *Example:* Heavy rain can hinder the bus service in the morning.

9. **Impartial** (im-PAR-shul) — Treating everyone equally; not favoring one side.
   *Example:* The judges remained impartial while scoring every team.

10. **Jubilant** (JOO-bih-lunt) — Feeling or showing great happiness and triumph.
    *Example:* The jubilant crowd cheered when their school won the trophy.

11. **Lucid** (LOO-sid) — Expressed clearly; easy to understand.
    *Example:* Her lucid summary helped the class understand the difficult chapter.

12. **Momentous** (moh-MEN-tus) — Very important, especially because of future effects.
    *Example:* Graduation day is a momentous occasion for every student.

13. **Notorious** (noh-TOR-ee-us) — Famous for something bad.
    *Example:* The narrow bridge is notorious for causing traffic jams.

14. **Optimum** (OP-tih-mum) — The best or most favorable amount or condition.
    *Example:* Eight hours of sleep gives most students the optimum rest before exams.

15. **Prudent** (PROO-dent) — Careful and sensible when making decisions.
    *Example:* It is prudent to save some pocket money each month.

16. **Quell** (KWEL) — To stop or calm something, such as fear or trouble.
    *Example:* The captain's calm words helped quell the team's nervousness.

17. **Rigorous** (RIG-er-us) — Extremely thorough, careful, or strict.
    *Example:* The school follows a rigorous schedule during exam season.

18. **Stagnant** (STAG-nunt) — Not moving, developing, or changing; still.
    *Example:* The stagnant pond water attracted mosquitoes near the field.

19. **Trivial** (TRIV-ee-ul) — Of little value or importance.
    *Example:* They argued over a trivial detail instead of the main plan.

20. **Vigilant** (VIJ-ih-lunt) — Keeping careful watch for possible danger or trouble.
    *Example:* The lifeguard stayed vigilant while the children swam.
