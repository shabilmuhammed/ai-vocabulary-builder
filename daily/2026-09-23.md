# Today's Words — 2026-09-23

1. **Abstain** (ab-STAYN) — To choose not to do something, especially by holding back or voting no.
   *Example:* He decided to abstain from junk food during exam week.
2. **Adverse** (AD-vurss) — Harmful or unfavorable; working against something.
   *Example:* The heavy rain had an adverse effect on the sports day schedule.
3. **Ambivalent** (am-BIV-uh-lent) — Having mixed feelings about something; unsure.
   *Example:* She felt ambivalent about switching schools, excited yet nervous.
4. **Anecdote** (AN-ik-doht) — A short, interesting story about a real event or person.
   *Example:* The teacher shared a funny anecdote about her own school days.
5. **Apathy** (AP-uh-thee) — A lack of interest or concern about something.
   *Example:* The coach was worried about the team's apathy after losing the match.
6. **Articulate** (ar-TIK-yuh-lit) — Able to express thoughts and ideas clearly.
   *Example:* The class president is articulate and always explains her ideas well.
7. **Assertive** (uh-SUR-tiv) — Confident and direct in stating your opinions or needs.
   *Example:* Being assertive helped her ask the teacher for extra help without hesitation.
8. **Astute** (uh-STOOT) — Very clever and quick to notice things, especially in tricky situations.
   *Example:* The astute detective in the story solved the mystery before anyone else.
9. **Authentic** (aw-THEN-tik) — Real and genuine, not copied or fake.
   *Example:* The museum displayed authentic artifacts from ancient Egypt.
10. **Belligerent** (buh-LIJ-er-ent) — Aggressive and eager to argue or fight.
    *Example:* The belligerent player was given a warning for shouting at the referee.
11. **Boisterous** (BOY-stur-us) — Noisy, energetic, and full of high spirits.
    *Example:* The boisterous crowd cheered loudly when the team scored.
12. **Brevity** (BREV-ih-tee) — The quality of being short and to the point.
    *Example:* The judges praised the speech for its brevity and clear message.
13. **Callous** (KAL-us) — Not caring about other people's feelings; unkind.
    *Example:* It was callous of him to laugh at his friend's mistake.
14. **Coerce** (koh-URSS) — To force someone to do something they don't want to do.
    *Example:* No one should coerce a classmate into copying homework.
15. **Complacent** (kum-PLAY-sunt) — Too satisfied with yourself to notice problems or try harder.
    *Example:* After winning easily, the team became complacent and lost the next game.
16. **Conducive** (kun-DOO-siv) — Making a certain situation or outcome likely or possible.
    *Example:* A quiet room is conducive to good studying.
17. **Congenial** (kun-JEEN-yul) — Friendly and pleasant to be around.
    *Example:* The new teacher has a congenial manner that puts students at ease.
18. **Conscientious** (kon-shee-EN-shus) — Careful to do things correctly and thoroughly.
    *Example:* A conscientious student always checks their homework before submitting it.
19. **Cordial** (KOR-jul) — Warm and friendly, especially in a polite way.
    *Example:* The two rival teams shook hands in a cordial manner after the match.
20. **Cynical** (SIN-ih-kul) — Believing that people are mainly selfish and not to be trusted.
    *Example:* He felt cynical about the new rule, doubting it would actually help students.
