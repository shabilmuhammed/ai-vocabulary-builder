# Today's Words — 2026-09-19

1. **Adamant** (AD-uh-mant) — Refusing to change one's mind; firmly determined.
   *Example:* She was adamant that she would finish the race despite the pain.

2. **Bewilder** (bih-WIL-der) — To confuse someone greatly.
   *Example:* The complicated map bewildered the new students on their first day.

3. **Deft** (DEFT) — Skillful and quick in movement or action.
   *Example:* The chef's deft hands sliced the vegetables in seconds.

4. **Earnest** (UR-nist) — Sincere and serious in intention or effort.
   *Example:* He made an earnest attempt to apologize to his friend.

5. **Flourish** (FLUR-ish) — To grow or develop successfully; to thrive.
   *Example:* The plants flourish in the sunny corner of the garden.

6. **Gullible** (GUL-ih-bul) — Easily tricked or deceived.
   *Example:* He is so gullible that he believed the obvious prank.

7. **Haphazard** (hap-HAZ-erd) — Lacking order or planning; random.
   *Example:* The books were arranged in a haphazard way on the shelf.

8. **Indifferent** (in-DIF-er-ent) — Not interested or concerned about something.
   *Example:* She seemed indifferent to the outcome of the match.

9. **Jargon** (JAR-gun) — Special words or terms used by a particular group.
   *Example:* The doctors used medical jargon that confused the patients.

10. **Kindle** (KIN-dul) — To start a fire, or to arouse a feeling or interest.
    *Example:* The teacher's story helped kindle a love of reading in her students.

11. **Languish** (LANG-gwish) — To become weak or fail to make progress.
    *Example:* Without water, the plants began to languish in the heat.

12. **Meddle** (MED-ul) — To interfere in something that is not one's concern.
    *Example:* He always tries to meddle in his sister's arguments.

13. **Nurture** (NUR-cher) — To care for and help something grow or develop.
    *Example:* Good teachers nurture their students' curiosity.

14. **Optimistic** (op-tih-MIS-tik) — Hopeful and confident about the future.
    *Example:* She stayed optimistic even after failing the first attempt.

15. **Persistent** (per-SIS-tent) — Continuing firmly despite difficulty or opposition.
    *Example:* His persistent practice finally paid off in the final match.

16. **Query** (KWEER-ee) — A question, especially one expressing doubt.
    *Example:* The student raised a query about the homework instructions.

17. **Radiant** (RAY-dee-unt) — Shining brightly, or glowing with happiness.
    *Example:* Her radiant smile lit up the whole room.

18. **Skeptical** (SKEP-tih-kul) — Having doubts; not easily convinced.
    *Example:* He was skeptical about the rumor going around school.

19. **Tranquil** (TRANG-kwil) — Calm and peaceful, free from disturbance.
    *Example:* The lake looked tranquil in the early morning light.

20. **Utilize** (YOO-tih-lyze) — To make practical use of something.
    *Example:* Students should utilize the library resources before exams.

21. **Vibrant** (VY-brant) — Full of energy and life; bright and colorful.
    *Example:* The market was vibrant with colors and sounds.

22. **Whimsical** (WIM-zih-kul) — Playful, unusual, and slightly unpredictable.
    *Example:* The story had a whimsical character who talked to animals.

23. **Yearn** (YURN) — To have a strong desire or longing for something.
    *Example:* After months away, she began to yearn for home.

24. **Zenith** (ZEE-nith) — The highest point or peak of something.
    *Example:* The team reached the zenith of its success this season.

25. **Ambiguous** (am-BIG-yoo-us) — Having more than one possible meaning; unclear.
    *Example:* His ambiguous answer left the class confused.

26. **Boisterous** (BOY-ster-us) — Noisy, energetic, and full of high spirits.
    *Example:* The boisterous crowd cheered loudly at the game.

27. **Concise** (kun-SYCE) — Giving information clearly and in few words.
    *Example:* Her concise summary made the long chapter easy to understand.

28. **Dubious** (DOO-bee-us) — Doubtful or uncertain about something.
    *Example:* He gave a dubious look when told the test was cancelled.

29. **Enhance** (en-HANS) — To improve the quality or value of something.
    *Example:* Extra practice can enhance your writing skills.

30. **Fickle** (FIK-ul) — Changing often, especially in loyalty or feelings.
    *Example:* The weather has been fickle all week, sunny then rainy.

31. **Gregarious** (grih-GAIR-ee-us) — Sociable and enjoying the company of others.
    *Example:* Her gregarious nature makes her popular at school.

32. **Hamper** (HAM-per) — To prevent or slow down the progress of something.
    *Example:* Heavy traffic can hamper the school bus schedule.

33. **Intricate** (IN-trih-kit) — Very detailed and complicated.
    *Example:* The artist drew an intricate pattern on the wall.

34. **Jeopardize** (JEP-er-dyze) — To put something at risk of harm or loss.
    *Example:* Skipping practice could jeopardize his place on the team.

35. **Ponder** (PON-der) — To think carefully about something for a while.
    *Example:* She sat by the window to ponder her decision.
