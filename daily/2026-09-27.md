# Today's Words — 2026-09-27

1. **Aberrant** (uh-BER-unt) — Different from what is normal or expected, usually in a bad way.
   *Example:* The teacher noticed the student's aberrant behavior and asked if something was wrong.
2. **Adjacent** (uh-JAY-sunt) — Next to or beside something.
   *Example:* The library is adjacent to the school's main building.
3. **Advocate** (AD-vuh-kayt) — To publicly support or argue for a cause or idea.
   *Example:* She likes to advocate for cleaner parks in her neighborhood.
4. **Affluent** (AF-loo-unt) — Having a lot of money; wealthy.
   *Example:* The affluent family donated a large sum to the school's new library.
5. **Alleviate** (uh-LEE-vee-ayt) — To make a problem or pain less severe.
   *Example:* Drinking water helped alleviate her headache before the exam.
6. **Amenable** (uh-MEE-nuh-bul) — Willing to agree with or accept something.
   *Example:* He was amenable to changing the meeting time so everyone could attend.
7. **Animosity** (an-ih-MOSS-ih-tee) — A strong feeling of dislike or hostility.
   *Example:* Years of animosity between the two rival teams made the final match tense.
8. **Appease** (uh-PEEZ) — To calm someone down by giving them what they want.
   *Example:* The manager offered a refund to appease the unhappy customer.
9. **Apprehensive** (ap-rih-HEN-siv) — Feeling worried or nervous about something that might happen.
   *Example:* She felt apprehensive before walking into her first driving test.
10. **Bemoan** (bih-MOHN) — To express sadness or complaint about something.
    *Example:* The students would bemoan the amount of homework given every Friday.
11. **Buoyant** (BOY-unt) — Cheerful and full of energy; able to stay afloat.
    *Example:* Her buoyant mood lifted everyone's spirits during the long trip.
12. **Capitulate** (kuh-PICH-oo-layt) — To stop resisting and give in to someone's demands.
    *Example:* After a long argument, he finally capitulated and agreed to clean his room.
13. **Contentious** (kun-TEN-shus) — Likely to cause an argument or disagreement.
    *Example:* Whether to wear school uniforms remained a contentious topic among students.
14. **Deference** (DEF-er-uns) — Respectful behavior shown toward someone, especially an elder or expert.
    *Example:* The students showed deference to the visiting principal by standing quietly.
15. **Deride** (dih-RYD) — To mock or make fun of someone in an unkind way.
    *Example:* It is unkind to deride a classmate for making a small mistake.
16. **Discreet** (dih-SKREET) — Careful to avoid embarrassing or drawing attention to someone.
    *Example:* She was discreet about her friend's bad grade and told no one.
17. **Elicit** (ih-LISS-it) — To draw out a response or information from someone.
    *Example:* The teacher asked open questions to elicit ideas from the quiet students.
18. **Emphatic** (em-FAT-ik) — Expressing something with force and clear conviction.
    *Example:* He gave an emphatic "no" when asked if he wanted to skip practice.
19. **Exacerbate** (ig-ZAS-er-bayt) — To make a problem or situation worse.
    *Example:* Skipping revision the night before will only exacerbate exam stress.
20. **Fastidious** (fas-TID-ee-us) — Very careful and attentive to small details; hard to please.
    *Example:* The fastidious editor checked every sentence twice for mistakes.
