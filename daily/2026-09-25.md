# Today's Words — 2026-09-25

1. **Quench** (KWENCH) — To satisfy your thirst, or to put out a fire.
   *Example:* She drank a glass of cold water to quench her thirst after the race.
2. **Rampant** (RAM-punt) — Spreading quickly and widely, often in an uncontrolled way.
   *Example:* Rumors spread rampant through the school before the exam results were posted.
3. **Scrutinize** (SKROO-tuh-nyze) — To examine something very closely and carefully.
   *Example:* The teacher scrutinized every line of the essay for spelling mistakes.
4. **Tangible** (TAN-juh-bul) — Able to be touched or clearly seen; real and definite.
   *Example:* Winning the trophy was tangible proof of the team's hard work.
5. **Unravel** (un-RAV-ul) — To undo or untangle something, or to solve a mystery.
   *Example:* The detective worked patiently to unravel the strange case.
6. **Vindicate** (VIN-dih-kayt) — To prove that someone was right or innocent after doubt.
   *Example:* The test results vindicated her original answer.
7. **Wholesome** (HOHL-sum) — Good for your health, or morally good and decent.
   *Example:* The school canteen now serves more wholesome meals.
8. **Yield** (YEELD) — To produce a result, or to give way under pressure.
   *Example:* Careful studying will yield better exam results.
9. **Zenith** (ZEE-nith) — The highest point or peak of something.
   *Example:* The singer reached the zenith of her career after the world tour.
10. **Abrupt** (uh-BRUPT) — Sudden and unexpected.
    *Example:* The bus came to an abrupt stop when the light turned red.
11. **Brisk** (BRISK) — Quick and energetic.
    *Example:* They took a brisk walk around the field before class started.
12. **Coax** (KOHKS) — To gently persuade someone to do something.
    *Example:* She coaxed her little brother into finishing his homework.
13. **Daunting** (DAWN-ting) — Seeming difficult or frightening to deal with.
    *Example:* The final exam felt daunting, but she prepared well.
14. **Exemplary** (ig-ZEM-pluh-ree) — Serving as an excellent example; outstanding.
    *Example:* His exemplary behavior earned him the class monitor badge.
15. **Flair** (FLAIR) — A natural talent or stylish quality.
    *Example:* She has a flair for painting colorful murals.
16. **Gauge** (GAYJ) — To measure or judge something carefully.
    *Example:* The coach asked questions to gauge how ready the team felt.
17. **Hindsight** (HYND-syte) — Understanding an event clearly only after it has happened.
    *Example:* In hindsight, he should have started studying earlier.
18. **Illuminate** (ih-LOO-muh-nayt) — To light something up, or to make an idea clear.
    *Example:* The lamp helped illuminate the dark study room.
19. **Knack** (NAK) — A special skill or ability to do something easily.
    *Example:* She has a knack for solving puzzles quickly.
20. **Lavish** (LAV-ish) — Very generous or luxurious.
    *Example:* The school held a lavish celebration for its 50th anniversary.
