# Today's Words — 2026-10-25

1. **Vacuous** (VAK-yoo-us) — Showing no intelligence or serious thought; empty-headed.
   *Example:* His vacuous stare made it obvious he hadn't listened to a single word.
   *Blank:* His expression turned utterly ___ during the boring lecture. | Mettle, Redress, Plethora
   *Synonym:* mindless | thoughtful, sharp, clever
   *Opposite:* thoughtful | mindless, empty, dull
   *Clue:* Describes a look or comment that shows no real thought behind it. | Sublime, Reverence, Mettle

2. **Mettle** (MET-ul) — Courage and strength of character, especially when things are difficult.
   *Example:* The firefighter's mettle was tested when she ran back into the burning house.
   *Blank:* The young athlete displayed real ___ when she kept racing despite the injury. | Vacuous, Precipitate, Superfluous
   *Synonym:* courage | cowardice, laziness, boredom
   *Opposite:* cowardice | courage, bravery, boldness
   *Clue:* The inner strength and courage someone shows when things get tough. | Panacea, Rife, Surmise

3. **Panacea** (pan-uh-SEE-uh) — Something imagined to be a perfect solution for every problem.
   *Example:* Many people wrongly think a single diet pill can be a panacea for weight loss.
   *Blank:* Many believed the new vaccine would act as ___ for the disease, but it only helped some patients. | Rife, Succumb, Innate
   *Synonym:* cure-all | disease, problem, failure
   *Clue:* Something wrongly believed to fix every kind of problem. | Mettle, Rife, Succumb

4. **Parity** (PAIR-ih-tee) — The state of being equal, especially in status, pay, or treatment.
   *Example:* The new law aims to bring parity between men's and women's sports funding.
   *Blank:* After years of unequal pay, the two departments finally achieved ___ in their salaries. | Reverence, Sublime, Surmise
   *Synonym:* equality | difference, chaos, debt
   *Opposite:* inequality | equality, balance, fairness
   *Clue:* The condition of two things being treated exactly the same. | Sage, Stymie, Vacuous

5. **Plethora** (PLETH-er-uh) — A very large amount of something, often more than is needed.
   *Example:* The museum has a plethora of ancient artifacts from different civilizations.
   *Blank:* Volunteers brought ___ of supplies to the shelter after the storm. | Sage, Stymie, Innate
   *Synonym:* abundance | shortage, absence, scarcity
   *Opposite:* scarcity | abundance, surplus, excess
   *Clue:* An extremely large, almost overwhelming quantity of something. | Reverence, Redress, Innate

6. **Precipitate** (pri-SIP-ih-tayt) — To cause something, usually something bad, to happen suddenly.
   *Example:* The sudden price hike helped precipitate protests across the city.
   *Blank:* Harsh new tariffs could ___ a trade war between the two nations. | Mettle, Rife, Parity
   *Synonym:* trigger | delay, prevent, soothe
   *Opposite:* prevent | trigger, cause, provoke
   *Clue:* To make a serious event happen suddenly and unexpectedly. | Parity, Solicitous, Sage

7. **Proclivity** (pro-KLIV-ih-tee) — A natural tendency to behave or think in a particular way.
   *Example:* Even as a child, he showed a proclivity for solving math puzzles.
   *Blank:* The scientist's ___ for detail made her experiments remarkably accurate. | Succumb, Solicitous, Redress
   *Synonym:* tendency | ability, memory, doubt
   *Opposite:* aversion | tendency, inclination, habit
   *Clue:* A natural leaning toward behaving a certain way. | Panacea, Succumb, Vacuous

8. **Innate** (ih-NAYT) — Existing in someone from birth, rather than learned.
   *Example:* Some scientists believe curiosity is an innate trait found in all babies.
   *Blank:* Many musicians seem to have ___ talent for rhythm, as if born with it. | Panacea, Stymie, Reverence
   *Synonym:* inborn | learned, forced, copied
   *Opposite:* acquired | inborn, natural, inherent
   *Clue:* Present in someone from birth rather than learned later. | Rife, Parity, Redress

9. **Redress** (rih-DRESS) — To correct a wrong or make up for harm that was done.
   *Example:* The committee promised to redress the unfair treatment of the workers.
   *Blank:* The court ordered the company to ___ the harm caused to its workers. | Vacuous, Plethora, Mettle
   *Synonym:* remedy | ignore, worsen, deny
   *Opposite:* worsen | fix, remedy, correct
   *Clue:* To make up for a wrong by correcting or compensating for it. | Sublime, Mettle, Plethora

10. **Repudiate** (rih-PYOO-dee-ayt) — To refuse to accept, support, or be associated with something.
    *Example:* The author chose to repudiate the false claims made about her book.
    *Blank:* The senator chose to publicly ___ the extreme views expressed by his former ally. | Rife, Parity, Superfluous
    *Synonym:* reject | accept, support, praise
    *Opposite:* endorse | reject, deny, refuse
    *Clue:* To firmly refuse to accept or be connected with something. | Rife, Innate, Parity

11. **Resonate** (REZ-uh-nayt) — To have a strong emotional or meaningful effect on someone.
    *Example:* The message about kindness continued to resonate long after the assembly ended.
    *Blank:* Her speech about kindness seemed to ___ with everyone in the auditorium. | Panacea, Sage, Proclivity
    *Synonym:* strike a chord | confuse, bore, annoy
    *Opposite:* alienate | connect, inspire, move
    *Clue:* To create a strong feeling of connection or meaning in someone. | Sage, Proclivity, Panacea

12. **Reverence** (REV-er-ens) — Deep respect and admiration for someone or something.
    *Example:* Visitors spoke in whispers, showing reverence inside the ancient temple.
    *Blank:* The students bowed their heads in ___ before the memorial statue. | Succumb, Sublime, Precipitate
    *Synonym:* awe | mockery, anger, boredom
    *Opposite:* contempt | respect, admiration, awe
    *Clue:* A feeling of deep respect mixed with a little awe. | Succumb, Sublime, Precipitate

13. **Rife** (RYF) — Very common or widespread, usually describing something unwanted.
    *Example:* The abandoned building was rife with broken windows and graffiti.
    *Blank:* The old building was ___ with rumors of ghosts roaming its halls. | Mettle, Redress, Surmise
    *Synonym:* widespread | rare, hidden, calm
    *Opposite:* scarce | widespread, common, rampant
    *Clue:* Full of something, usually something unpleasant or unwanted. | Mettle, Redress, Surmise

14. **Sage** (SAYJ) — Wise, especially because of age and experience.
    *Example:* Grandfather's sage words helped her decide which college to attend.
    *Blank:* The old teacher gave ___ advice that the students never forgot. | Panacea, Stymie, Parity
    *Synonym:* wise | foolish, careless, confused
    *Opposite:* foolish | wise, clever, insightful
    *Clue:* Showing wisdom that usually comes from age and experience. | Panacea, Parity, Stymie

15. **Solicitous** (suh-LISS-ih-tus) — Showing care and concern for someone's comfort or needs.
    *Example:* The waiter was unusually solicitous, checking on us every few minutes.
    *Blank:* The nurse was always ___ about her patients' comfort and needs. | Plethora, Precipitate, Reverence
    *Synonym:* caring | careless, rude, indifferent
    *Opposite:* indifferent | caring, attentive, concerned
    *Clue:* Showing eager concern for someone's comfort or well-being. | Plethora, Reverence, Precipitate

16. **Stymie** (STY-mee) — To block or prevent progress on something.
    *Example:* A sudden power outage stymied the team's plan to finish the project on time.
    *Blank:* Heavy rain continued to ___ the rescue team's efforts to reach the village. | Innate, Rife, Vacuous
    *Synonym:* hinder | help, allow, speed
    *Opposite:* facilitate | hinder, block, obstruct
    *Clue:* To block or slow down someone's progress on purpose or by accident. | Innate, Rife, Vacuous

17. **Sublime** (suh-BLYM) — So beautiful, excellent, or impressive that it inspires awe.

    *Synonym:* magnificent | ordinary, ugly, dull
    *Opposite:* ordinary | magnificent, grand, glorious
    *Clue:* So beautiful or excellent that it fills you with wonder. | Redress, Mettle, Succumb

    *Synonym:* unnecessary | essential, rare, useful
    *Opposite:* essential | unnecessary, extra, excess
    *Clue:* More of something than is actually needed. | Plethora, Resonate, Panacea

    *Example:* From the muddy footprints, we could surmise that someone had entered through the garden.

    *Synonym:* guess | prove, deny, measure
    *Opposite:* confirm | guess, assume, suspect
    *Clue:* To work out a likely answer without having full proof. | Reverence, Innate, Proclivity
