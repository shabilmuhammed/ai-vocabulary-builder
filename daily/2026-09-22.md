# Today's Words — 2026-09-22

1. **Amiable** (AY-mee-uh-bul) — Friendly and pleasant; easy to like.
   *Example:* The new student was amiable and quickly made friends.
2. **Bolster** (BOHL-ster) — To support or strengthen something.
   *Example:* Extra practice will bolster your confidence before the exam.
3. **Credible** (KRED-uh-bul) — Believable; able to be trusted.
   *Example:* The witness gave a credible account of what happened.
4. **Discern** (dih-SURN) — To notice or understand something clearly, often with effort.
   *Example:* It was hard to discern the correct answer from the confusing question.
5. **Erratic** (ih-RAT-ik) — Not regular or predictable; changing suddenly.
   *Example:* The bus schedule has been erratic since the roadwork started.
6. **Futile** (FYOO-tyle) — Useless; having no effect no matter how hard you try.
   *Example:* Arguing with him about the rules was futile.
7. **Grueling** (GROO-uh-ling) — Very tiring and difficult.
   *Example:* The final exams were grueling after weeks of revision.
8. **Humane** (hyoo-MAYN) — Kind and caring, especially toward those who are suffering.
   *Example:* The vet was humane and gentle with the injured dog.
9. **Inevitable** (in-EV-ih-tuh-bul) — Certain to happen; impossible to avoid.
   *Example:* With no revision at all, a low mark was inevitable.
10. **Judicious** (joo-DISH-us) — Showing good judgment; sensible and careful.
    *Example:* She made a judicious decision to study instead of watching TV.
11. **Kudos** (KOO-dohss) — Praise given for an achievement.
    *Example:* The team received kudos for winning the science fair.
12. **Lethargic** (luh-THAR-jik) — Lacking energy; tired and slow to move.
    *Example:* After the long lunch, the class felt lethargic during the lesson.
13. **Meander** (mee-AN-der) — To wander slowly without a fixed direction.
    *Example:* The river meanders through the valley before reaching the sea.
14. **Nostalgic** (nos-TAL-jik) — Feeling a fond longing for the past.
    *Example:* Looking at old photos made her nostalgic for primary school.
15. **Onerous** (OH-ner-us) — Involving a lot of effort; burdensome.
    *Example:* Carrying all the textbooks home was an onerous task.
16. **Perceptive** (per-SEP-tiv) — Quick to notice or understand things.
    *Example:* The perceptive teacher noticed the student was upset before anyone else did.
17. **Quirky** (KWUR-kee) — Unusual in an interesting or amusing way.
    *Example:* His quirky sense of humor always made the class laugh.
18. **Robust** (roh-BUST) — Strong and healthy; not easily broken or weakened.
    *Example:* The bridge was built with a robust design to survive earthquakes.
19. **Solitude** (SOL-ih-tood) — The state of being alone, often by choice.
    *Example:* She enjoyed the solitude of the library while studying.
20. **Thrive** (THRYVE) — To grow or develop successfully; to flourish.
    *Example:* Plants thrive when they get enough sunlight and water.
