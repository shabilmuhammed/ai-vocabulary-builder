# Today's Words — 2026-09-18

1. **Meticulous** (meh-TIK-yoo-lus) — Very careful and paying great attention to detail.
   *Example:* She was meticulous when checking her exam paper for mistakes.

2. **Candid** (KAN-did) — Honest and direct in what you say.
   *Example:* The coach gave the team a candid opinion about their performance.

3. **Diligent** (DIL-ih-junt) — Working hard and carefully.
   *Example:* A diligent student finishes homework before watching TV.

4. **Eloquent** (EL-oh-kwent) — Able to speak or write fluently and persuasively.
   *Example:* Her eloquent speech convinced the whole class to join the project.

5. **Frugal** (FROO-gul) — Careful about spending money; not wasteful.
   *Example:* He is frugal and saves part of his pocket money every week.

6. **Genuine** (JEN-yoo-in) — Real and true, not fake.
   *Example:* Her apology sounded genuine, so her friend forgave her.

7. **Hasty** (HAY-stee) — Done too quickly, without enough thought.
   *Example:* He made a hasty decision and regretted it later.

8. **Immense** (ih-MENS) — Extremely large in size or amount.
   *Example:* The stadium was filled with an immense crowd for the final match.

9. **Jovial** (JOH-vee-ul) — Cheerful and friendly.
   *Example:* Our new teacher has a jovial personality that makes class fun.

10. **Keen** (KEEN) — Having a strong interest or enthusiasm.
    *Example:* She is keen to learn how computers work.

11. **Lenient** (LEE-nee-unt) — Not strict; forgiving when judging someone.
    *Example:* The teacher was lenient and gave the class extra time for the test.

12. **Meager** (MEE-ger) — Very small in amount; not enough.
    *Example:* The farmers had a meager harvest after the dry season.

13. **Novice** (NAH-vis) — A person who is new and inexperienced at something.
    *Example:* As a novice swimmer, he stayed near the shallow end.

14. **Obstinate** (OB-stih-nit) — Stubborn; refusing to change one's mind.
    *Example:* The obstinate goat would not move from the road.

15. **Plausible** (PLAW-zuh-bul) — Seeming reasonable or probably true.
    *Example:* His excuse for being late sounded plausible.

16. **Quaint** (KWAYNT) — Attractively old-fashioned or unusual.
    *Example:* They visited a quaint little village during the holidays.

17. **Reluctant** (rih-LUK-tunt) — Unwilling and hesitant to do something.
    *Example:* She was reluctant to speak in front of the whole school.

18. **Sincere** (sin-SEER) — Honest and true in feeling; not pretended.
    *Example:* He gave a sincere thank-you to his parents for their support.

19. **Tedious** (TEE-dee-us) — Boring and taking too long.
    *Example:* Copying the notes twice felt like a tedious task.

20. **Unanimous** (yoo-NAN-ih-mus) — Everyone agrees completely.
    *Example:* The class reached a unanimous decision to plant a garden.
