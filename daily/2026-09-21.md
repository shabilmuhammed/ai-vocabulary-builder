# Today's Words — 2026-09-21

1. **Ardent** (AR-dent) — Full of strong feeling; passionate and enthusiastic.
   *Example:* She is an ardent supporter of the school football team.

2. **Benign** (bih-NYNE) — Gentle and kind; not harmful.
   *Example:* The old dog had a benign nature and never scared visitors.

3. **Concise** (kun-SYSE) — Giving information clearly in a few words.
   *Example:* The teacher asked for a concise summary of the chapter.

4. **Dubious** (DOO-bee-us) — Feeling doubt or uncertainty about something.
   *Example:* He was dubious about the shortcut and decided to check the map.

5. **Elated** (ih-LAY-tid) — Extremely happy and excited.
   *Example:* She was elated when she saw her name on the honor roll.

6. **Fickle** (FIK-ul) — Changing opinions or loyalty often and without reason.
   *Example:* His fickle taste in music changes every single week.

7. **Gregarious** (grih-GAIR-ee-us) — Enjoying the company of others; sociable.
   *Example:* The gregarious new student quickly made friends with everyone.

8. **Hamper** (HAM-per) — To make it difficult for something to happen or progress.
   *Example:* The heavy traffic hampered our trip to the museum.

9. **Incessant** (in-SES-unt) — Continuing without stopping.
   *Example:* The incessant rain forced the match to be postponed.

10. **Jeopardize** (JEP-er-dyze) — To put something at risk of harm or loss.
    *Example:* Skipping practice could jeopardize her chance of making the team.

11. **Kinship** (KIN-ship) — A close connection or relationship, like family.
    *Example:* The students felt a strong kinship after working together all year.

12. **Lament** (luh-MENT) — To express sadness or regret about something.
    *Example:* The captain lamented the team's loss after the final whistle.

13. **Malleable** (MAL-ee-uh-bul) — Easily shaped, changed, or influenced.
    *Example:* Clay is malleable, so it can be molded into many shapes.

14. **Nonchalant** (non-shuh-LAHNT) — Calm and relaxed, as if not worried or interested.
    *Example:* He stayed nonchalant even after hearing the surprising news.

15. **Obsolete** (ob-suh-LEET) — No longer used because something newer exists.
    *Example:* Old floppy disks are now obsolete in most computer labs.

16. **Pragmatic** (prag-MAT-ik) — Practical and sensible in solving problems.
    *Example:* She took a pragmatic approach to finishing the group project on time.

17. **Quandary** (KWON-duh-ree) — A state of confusion over what to do in a difficult situation.
    *Example:* He was in a quandary about which elective class to choose.

18. **Resilient** (rih-ZIL-yunt) — Able to recover quickly from difficulties.
    *Example:* The resilient athlete returned stronger after her injury.

19. **Succinct** (suk-SINKT) — Clearly expressed in a few words.
    *Example:* His succinct answer impressed the interviewer.

20. **Tentative** (TEN-tuh-tiv) — Not certain or fixed; done cautiously.
    *Example:* They made a tentative plan to meet at the library on Saturday.
