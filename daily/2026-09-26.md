# Today's Words — 2026-09-26

1. **Abate** (uh-BAYT) — To become less strong or severe; to lessen.
   *Example:* The storm began to abate after midnight, and the winds grew calm.
2. **Acquiesce** (ak-wee-ESS) — To accept something without arguing, even if you don't fully agree.
   *Example:* After a long debate, she reluctantly acquiesced to her teacher's request.
3. **Aloof** (uh-LOOF) — Distant or unfriendly; not wanting to join others.
   *Example:* The new student stayed aloof and rarely spoke to his classmates.
4. **Anomaly** (uh-NOM-uh-lee) — Something that is different from what is normal or expected.
   *Example:* A sudden snowfall in summer was a strange anomaly that surprised everyone.
5. **Arbitrary** (AR-bih-trair-ee) — Based on random choice rather than any reason or system.
   *Example:* The coach's arbitrary decision to bench the star player confused the whole team.
6. **Ascertain** (as-er-TAYN) — To find out something for certain.
   *Example:* The scientist ran several tests to ascertain the cause of the reaction.
7. **Auspicious** (aw-SPISH-us) — Showing signs of future success; favorable.
   *Example:* Winning the first match was an auspicious start to the tournament.
8. **Belated** (bih-LAY-tid) — Coming or happening later than expected.
   *Example:* He sent his sister a belated birthday card a week after her birthday.
9. **Cajole** (kuh-JOHL) — To persuade someone gently by praising or flattering them.
   *Example:* She tried to cajole her brother into lending her his bicycle.
10. **Copious** (KOH-pee-us) — Existing in large amounts; plentiful.
    *Example:* The students took copious notes before the final exam.
11. **Denounce** (dih-NOWNSS) — To publicly criticize or accuse someone of doing something wrong.
    *Example:* The principal denounced cheating during the assembly speech.
12. **Digress** (dye-GRESS) — To move away from the main topic when speaking or writing.
    *Example:* The teacher often digressed into old stories before returning to the lesson.
13. **Eccentric** (ek-SEN-trik) — Unusual or strange in a harmless, sometimes amusing way.
    *Example:* Our eccentric neighbor collects old clocks from every country he visits.
14. **Emulate** (EM-yoo-layt) — To try to be like someone you admire by copying their actions.
    *Example:* Young athletes often emulate the training habits of their favorite players.
15. **Extol** (ek-STOHL) — To praise someone or something highly.
    *Example:* The coach extolled the team's teamwork after their surprising win.
16. **Facetious** (fuh-SEE-shus) — Joking about something in a way that is not appropriate for the moment.
    *Example:* His facetious remark during the serious meeting made the teacher frown.
17. **Frivolous** (FRIV-uh-lus) — Not serious or sensible; wasting time or money on unimportant things.
    *Example:* Spending all her allowance on frivolous toys left her with no savings.
18. **Impeccable** (im-PEK-uh-bul) — Perfect; without any mistakes or flaws.
    *Example:* Her impeccable handwriting impressed every teacher who graded her work.
19. **Inquisitive** (in-KWIZ-uh-tiv) — Very curious and eager to learn or ask questions.
    *Example:* The inquisitive child asked her parents why the sky changes color at sunset.
20. **Nebulous** (NEB-yoo-lus) — Unclear, vague, or hard to define.
    *Example:* His explanation of the plan was so nebulous that nobody understood the next step.
