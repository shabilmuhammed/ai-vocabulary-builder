# Today's Words — 2026-11-23

1. **Vernacular** (ver-NAK-yoo-lar) — The everyday, informal language ordinary people speak, rather than formal or technical language.
   *Example:* The old poem was written in plain vernacular so villagers could understand it easily.
   *Blank:* Local comedians often get bigger laughs when they joke in the ___ instead of using stiff, formal English. | Gainsay, Sedulous, Remonstrate
   *Synonym:* everyday language | Formal speech, Ancient script, Secret code
   *Opposite:* formal language | Everyday talk, Casual chat, Slang
   *Clue:* The ordinary, informal form of language that local people actually speak day to day. | Stipend, Quietude, Surfeit

2. **Sedulous** (SEJ-oo-lus) — Showing great care, effort, and steady persistence in work.
   *Example:* Her sedulous attention to every detail made the science project flawless.
   *Blank:* The ___ research assistant double-checked every footnote three times before submitting the report. | Stipend, Remonstrate, Tautology
   *Synonym:* diligent | Lazy, Careless, Hasty
   *Opposite:* lazy | Diligent, Careful, Thorough
   *Clue:* Marked by steady effort and painstaking care put into a task. | Slovenly, Soporific, Stolid

3. **Stipend** (STY-pend) — A fixed, regular sum of money paid as an allowance, often to a student or trainee.
   *Example:* The intern received a small stipend to cover her travel costs each month.
   *Blank:* Each volunteer teacher at the summer camp received a modest ___ to cover bus fare and lunch. | Gainsay, Sedulous, Remonstrate
   *Synonym:* allowance | Penalty, Debt, Discount
   *Clue:* A regular, fixed sum of money given as support, often to a student or trainee. | Lassitude, Tautology, Quietude

4. **Gainsay** (GAYN-say) — To deny or speak against a statement; to contradict.
   *Example:* No one dared to gainsay the captain's decision during the final over.
   *Blank:* Few people chose to ___ the judge's ruling once the evidence had been presented. | Plenary, Inchoate, Lassitude
   *Synonym:* contradict | Agree, Support, Praise
   *Opposite:* agree | Contradict, Deny, Argue
   *Clue:* To speak against a claim and declare it to be false. | Supplant, Remonstrate, Disabuse

5. **Insuperable** (in-SOO-per-uh-bul) — Impossible to overcome; too great to be dealt with successfully.
   *Example:* The flooded river seemed like an insuperable obstacle until the villagers built a raft.

   *Synonym:* unbeatable | Easy, Simple, Weak
   *Opposite:* manageable | Unbeatable, Overwhelming, Impossible
   *Clue:* So great or difficult that it seems impossible to get past. | Soporific, Slovenly, Plenary

6. **Disabuse** (dis-uh-BYOOZ) — To free someone from a mistaken belief by telling them the truth.
   *Example:* The coach had to disabuse the new players of the idea that practice was optional.
   *Blank:* The guide tried to ___ the tourists of the idea that the ancient ruins were haunted. | Scurrilous, Surfeit, Redoubtable
   *Synonym:* correct | Confuse, Deceive, Flatter
   *Opposite:* mislead | Correct, Enlighten, Inform
   *Clue:* To correct someone by removing a false idea they believed. | Gainsay, Supplant, Remonstrate

7. **Inchoate** (in-KOH-it) — Just beginning and not yet fully formed or developed.
   *Example:* Her plan for the school festival was still inchoate, with many details missing.

   *Synonym:* unformed | Finished, Perfect, Detailed
   *Opposite:* complete | Unformed, Early, Rough
   *Clue:* At an early, rough stage, without a clear or finished shape yet. | Redoubtable, Temperate, Plenary

8. **Lassitude** (LASS-ih-tood) — A feeling of tiredness and lack of energy or interest.
   *Example:* A deep lassitude settled over the players after the long, hot match.

   *Synonym:* fatigue | Excitement, Strength, Hunger
   *Opposite:* energy | Fatigue, Exhaustion, Weariness
   *Clue:* A heavy sense of tiredness and low energy after great effort. | Surfeit, Vernacular, Tautology

9. **Plenary** (PLEE-nuh-ree) — Complete and full, especially describing a meeting attended by all members.

    *Clue:* A settled, peaceful stillness after noise or activity has stopped. | Surfeit, Lassitude, Tautology

    *Clue:* To speak out firmly in protest against a decision or action. | Supplant, Gainsay, Disabuse
